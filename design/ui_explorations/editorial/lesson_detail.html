Self-Brief

Changing the aeroplane's vertical path is not a matter of pulling back to climb or pushing forward to descend. The elevator is, primarily, a speed control. To climb, we add power — and the aeroplane climbs. To descend, we reduce power — and it descends.

The aeroplane is in a state of balance in straight and level flight, and the climb is simply the result of disturbing that balance with excess thrust. Pitch attitude follows; trim follows attitude.

“Power for performance, attitude for airspeed, trim for hands-off.” — PPL Confidential, A. Birch

Practically: from straight and level cruise, smoothly apply full throttle (be wary of yaw — right rudder, in our case), check power, raise the nose to the climb attitude — about three fingers above the horizon in a PA-28 — and trim. Lookout. The world has not stopped because you are climbing.

Flashcards

3 of 12

What controls airspeed in a stable climb?

Pitch attitude. Power is set; the elevator is used to attain and hold the climb attitude, which determines IAS.

Why right rudder on the climb-out?

Slipstream effect, asymmetric blade effect (P-factor) and torque all yaw the nose left at high power, low IAS, high pitch.

Best Rate (Vy) and Best Angle (Vx) — PA-28-161?

Vy is roughly 79 KIAS, Vx around 64 KIAS. Vy gives most height per minute; Vx most height per ground distance.
