What's the difference between a stall and a spin recovery, and why are we taught them as separate exercises?
The Tutor

A useful question — and the cleanest way to think about it is in terms of what's failed.

A stall is a loss of lift caused by the angle of attack exceeding the critical angle. The wing is still flying, symmetrically; recovery is about reducing the angle of attack (lower the nose), keep the wings level with rudder, and apply power as appropriate.

A spin follows a stall when one wing is more deeply stalled than the other — most commonly because of yaw at the stall. The aircraft auto-rotates. Recovery is the standard PARE drill: Power idle, Ailerons neutral, Rudder full opposite, Elevator forward to break the stall.

They're taught separately because the cures don't overlap — aileron in a spin will deepen it. The stall lesson teaches you to not yaw; the spin lesson teaches you what to do when, despite that, you have.

And on a PA-28 — is full spin recovery part of my PPL syllabus?
The Tutor

For the UK PPL — no, not in the PA-28. The syllabus requires incipient spin recognition and recovery, not the developed spin. The PA-28-161 is also placarded against intentional spinning.

You'll be shown the entry conditions (yawed stall) and recover before the rotation establishes — a few seconds of departure, not multiple turns. It's Ex. 11A in the syllabus, after stalling.
