What's the difference between a stall and a spin recovery, and why are we taught them as separate exercises?
FP
The Tutor
09:38

The cleanest way to think about it is in terms of what's failed.

A stall is a loss of lift caused by exceeding the critical angle of attack. The wing is still flying symmetrically. Recovery: reduce angle of attack (lower the nose), wings level with rudder, apply power as appropriate.

A spin follows a stall when one wing is more deeply stalled than the other — usually from yaw at the stall. The aircraft auto-rotates. Recovery is the standard PARE drill:

PARE Drill — Spin Recovery

Power idle · Ailerons neutral · Rudder full opposite to rotation · Elevator forward to break the stall

Taught separately because the cures don't overlap — aileron in a spin will deepen it. Stall lesson: don't yaw. Spin lesson: what to do when, despite that, you have.

And on a PA-28 — is full spin recovery part of my PPL syllabus?

For the UK PPL — no, not in the PA-28. The syllabus requires incipient spin recognition and recovery, not the developed spin.

The PA-28-161 is also placarded against intentional spinning. You'll be shown the entry conditions (yawed stall) and recover before the rotation establishes — a few seconds of departure, not multiple turns. Ex. 11A, after stalling.
