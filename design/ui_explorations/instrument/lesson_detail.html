Self-Brief

Changing the aeroplane's vertical path is not done with the elevator alone. Power for performance, attitude for airspeed, trim for hands-off. The elevator is, primarily, a speed control.

To climb, add power and the aeroplane climbs. Pitch attitude then determines IAS. To descend, reduce power. The aeroplane descends; pitch attitude again determines IAS.

PA-28-161 · Reference Speeds G-BOTU
  • Best Rate of Climb (Vy)79 KIAS
  • Best Angle of Climb (Vx)64 KIAS
  • Cruise Climb85 KIAS
  • Approach (flaps 25)75 KIAS
  • Glide (best L/D)73 KIAS

On the climb-out, expect yaw to the left from slipstream effect, asymmetric blade effect (P-factor) and torque — all maximised at high power · low IAS · high pitch. Apply right rudder.

Lookout matters more, not less, in the climb. Nose-up attitude hides the air ahead. Lower the nose periodically to clear the sector before continuing.

Flashcards

01 / 12

What controls airspeed in a stable climb?

Pitch attitude. Power is set; the elevator is used to attain and hold the climb attitude, which determines IAS.

02 / 12

Why right rudder on the climb-out?

Slipstream effect, P-factor and torque all yaw the nose left at high power · low IAS · high pitch. Right rudder keeps the slip-skid ball centred.

03 / 12

Carb heat on the descent — when?

At the start of any extended power reduction. Reduced airflow and lower MAP make the carburettor a candidate for icing in the cruise descent.
