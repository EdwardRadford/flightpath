Round 4 - new

Three more directions in the same hand-drawn ink language

Same brush-stroke energy, ink-bead device, cream paper, single accent. Three new subjects: a propeller caught mid-rotation, a windsock, and a PA-28 control yoke. None repeat any prior round. New typeface for each wordmark - Bodoni Moda, Young Serif, EB Garamond.

Concept 08

Propeller in motion

Three overlapping curved brushstrokes radiating from a central ink-bead hub - the blur of a moving propeller, captured as gesture rather than diagram. Unambiguously aviation: there is no other thing this could be. Each blade is a tapered closed-fill brush-shape with a softer inner ghost stroke (a second pulse of pigment, the place a real brush would cross itself). The hub IS the round-2 ink-bead device, repurposed as the spinner. Reads cleanly at favicon scale because the three-blade asymmetric Y-form is unmistakable even when tiny. Wordmark in Bodoni Moda - high-contrast modern serif, crisp and confident, pairs well with the spinning energy of the mark. Accent: deep prop-blue #1E3A8A.

Concept 09 REFINED v5 - POLE SHORTENED

Windsock

Round 5 makes one targeted change: the pole is shorter. v4's mast spanned 742 px against a 300 px cone (ratio ~2.49:1) and dominated the canvas. v5 trims the mast to 495 px so the pole : cone ratio is 1.65 : 1, sitting at the centre of the requested 1.5-1.8 window. The pole still reads as a windsock pole - visibly taller than the cone - but no longer eats two thirds of the height. Concretely, the mast bottom moves from path-source y=890 to y=643, and the ground anchors slide up to y=633..649 at the new base. The cone, cross-bar, suspension cords, ink-bead pivot, 5-band cone with continuous ink rim, rounded tail, and brush character are all untouched from v4. The artwork bbox is re-centred: vertical translate goes from 3.61 to 126.50 so the new shorter shape sits with equal top and bottom margins (~248 px each), while horizontal extents and translate (-2.98) are unchanged from v4. Cone still occupies the upper portion of the bbox. Accent stays red-orange #EA580C. Wordmark in Young Serif.

Concept 10

Control yoke

The W-shape of a Cessna / PA-28 control yoke, drawn loose. The most pilot-personal object in the cabin: it's what your hands actually hold for every minute of flight. Anyone who has flown a Warrior or a 172 reads this silhouette before they read any letter of the wordmark. The tan ink-bead at the centre is the trim / PTT hub; smaller tan beads mark the worn thumb-print spots on each horn where the leather grips darken from use. Risks: yoke is sometimes confused with a steering wheel at favicon scale, so the off-centre asymmetric horns and the short shaft below are doing critical work. Wordmark in EB Garamond - warm classical text serif, reads like a flight manual, intimate not shouty. Accent: tan #92400E (leather grip).
